The finding

The fog is not a brightness gradient. It is a chroma-and-variance veil — it flattens the map rather than darkening it.

Below the seam the map's luminance goes almost flat: 46–52 across a hundred rows, where above it swings 23–52 row to row. Colour drops by nearly three quarters. Mean brightness actually rises. The terrain is still visibly there — you can read the arc of buildings through the cards — but it has stopped competing.

That is why type can sit on it with no card behind it at all. And it is why the obvious build — a black-to-transparent gradient over the lower third — cannot reproduce this. That darkens; this calms.

04

Two glass registers, not one

Your brief says "glass capsules everywhere … translucent dark fill, a hairline of light on the edge." Half right. The reference runs two distinct capsule recipes, and the split is not decorative — it encodes hierarchy.

Register A · filled glass

Search field, the Measure/Locate/Print/Edit cluster, the icon circles. Translucent dark fill around #34383D, top-edge hairline lighter than the bottom, soft shadow beneath.

Meaning: permanent chrome. It is always there; it is allowed to occlude the map.
Register B · outline glass

Create / Reports / Actions, "Enter Resources", and both zoom buttons. A hairline stroke and no fill at all — the water reads straight through the interior.

Meaning: invoked actions. They refuse to hide the ground they sit on.
Also found

Every floating cluster sits on a local scrim — a soft radial darkening of the map behind it. The left control stack samples #05070B behind it while terrain 200 px to the right runs #38423A — 9.2× the luminance. That is not where the map happens to be dark; it is a deliberate legibility device, and it is how translucent chrome survives over live terrain. It goes straight into our accessibility floor.

08

What this evidence forces on the design

The fog must be built as a filter, not a fill.

Saturation and contrast reduction on the basemap layer itself, plus a blur plate — not a dark gradient over the top. This is the single most copyable fact on the page and the one most builds get wrong.

Our ground is a street map, so the green does not come with us.

The reference's terrain supplies its own mid-tones. Guntur's roads and water will not. Stage 2 has to decide what carries the mid-tone weight, or the composition collapses into black with floating boxes.

Ninety-two glows cannot be additive the way one ember is.

The reference blooms four or five points on a large dark field. Ours packs 92 into a city. Additive light at that density becomes a smear. The dot system has to solve density before it solves colour.
