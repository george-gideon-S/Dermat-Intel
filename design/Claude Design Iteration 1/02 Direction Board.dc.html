02

The one real risk · the vernier

A truncated axis is a data-visualisation sin. I am going to commit one deliberately, everywhere, as the product's core reading device — and I think the sin is on the other side.

The instrument
A vernier: the axis is cut to the market's own range, and every tick carries its own weight of evidence.
Window 3.5 → 5.0 tick weight = reviews
{{ v.rating }}
{{ v.reviews }}
3.5 5.0

Same ten clinics. The 5.0 on one review is now a hairline at the top of the scale; the 4.6 on 385 is a solid bar just left of it. The eye resolves high but unsupported from slightly lower and certain without a word of explanation.

Why it is a risk

Cutting an axis exaggerates differences. Done carelessly it turns a 0.3 gap into a chasm — which is precisely the accusatory register this product must never enter. If a doctor reads their bar as "half as good as my neighbour," the design has failed on the brief's central tension.

Why it serves the brief anyway

Three guards make it diagnostic rather than accusatory, and all three are enforceable in the guide:

01The window is always printed at both ends. A cut axis that declares its cut is an instrument setting, not a distortion.
02The window is the market's own range, never a designer's crop. It is a fact about Guntur, recomputed per snapshot and stored with it.
03No rating is ever drawn without its confidence. Position without weight is banned outright.
The defence, in one sentence

A scale that shows no difference where real difference exists is not neutral — it is the lie, and the five-star scale is already telling it.

04

The 92-glaze rule

Ninety-two distinct glows that read as one designed field. The rule is three lines long and reproducible exactly. Both fields below are the real 92 coordinates from the snapshot.
Fitted to full bounds · 33 km

This is the problem, not the answer. Fitting to the stated bounds puts 87 clinics inside a 4 km box and five strays across 33 km. The city dissolves into a single smear.
Fitted to the core · 4.5 km

The answer, and it amends your §5. Default view fits the interquartile core, not the extremes. The five outliers keep their rows and an off-frame bearing marker — never dropped, because a dropped clinic is a market finding that never happened.

The rule, reproducible exactly
Hold lightness and chroma constant. Walk only hue.
frac = ((i × 137.508) mod 360) / 360
H    = 50 + frac × 42
glaze = oklch(0.74 0.135 H)
The arc is 42° wide and entirely warm.
50°→92° renders #EE8F58 amber → #E09B3C gold → #C9A732 brass. Every sample sits at CSS hue 22–46°, so it never reaches red — which would read as alarm on a clinic that has done nothing wrong. Lightness is held at 0.74 rather than 0.68 because the top of the arc turns drab olive below that.
Map through the full circle, not the arc.
The obvious construction — (i × 137.508) mod 42 — is a trap. Modulo a narrow arc, the golden angle can land on a near-rational ratio and cycle: at a 50° arc it repeats every four clinics. Taking the fraction on 360° first keeps the sequence irrational at any arc width. Measured over 92 clinics as shipped: largest hue gap 1.20× the ideal spacing. Hue carries three decimal places — at one, a 42° arc holds only 421 steps and begins issuing duplicate glazes around 300 clinics, which would break the frozen-glaze promise the moment this runs on a larger market.
i is the clinic's alphabetical index, forever.
Not rank, not rating — those move between quarters. Snapshots are immutable, so a clinic's glaze must be too, or March and now are not comparable. Colour becomes an identity, and the doctor learns their own.
What encodes what
{{ e.channel }} {{ e.means }}

Pulse: 4.2 s period, opacity 0.55→1.0 and scale 0.94→1.06. Phase is staggered by the same golden angle, so at 92 dots the aggregate field is visually still — it breathes rather than strobes. Nothing pulses faster than 3 s; nothing pulses at all under reduced-motion.

06

Two competing wireframes

You asked me to choose between a map at half or a third, and justify it against the reference. I am choosing on a different basis: dot density. The reference is a proportion; ours is a constraint.

Wireframe A · chosen
The Seam · map 55 / list 45

One glass plate rises out of the fog; rows are readings on it, not cards. Nearest the seam they sit in the haze and resolve as they rise. Selection inverts to white — the reference's one loud move, spent here.

Wireframe B · rejected
The Ledger · map 33 / list 67

Drawn honestly so the rejection means something. It is the better information layout — more rows, less scrolling — and it fails on two counts. The map at 33% cannot separate 87 dots inside 4.5 km; they merge into the blob shown. And a stack of individually-bordered cards is exactly the wall of glass bricks your §4c warns about.

The justification is arithmetic, not taste
4.5 km

The core box holding 87 of 92 clinics.

540 px

Map height at 55% of a 1080 canvas, minus chrome.

18 px

Mean dot separation at that height. A 9 px dot clears its neighbour.

11 px

The same figure at 33%. Dots overlap, and glow makes it worse. This is the number that decided it.

08

The signature · and the thing it is not

The fog seam · committed, and inherited

Committed exactly as you asked, and built from Stage 1's measurement rather than the look of it: a chroma-and-variance veil on the basemap layer, −72% saturation, −39% luminance variance, +41% mean brightness, over the bottom 45% with a 13% band of pure atmosphere holding nothing.

But I won't call it our signature, because it is yours, and the reference's. Copying it faithfully is table stakes here — the brief is explicit and I agree with it. A signature has to be the thing no other product would arrive at.

Ours · the calibrated window

Every number in this product sits on a scale that shows its own window and its own weight of evidence.

Ratings, review counts, histograms, quarter-on-quarter movement — all of it on cut, declared, weighted axes. It is the product's actual argument made visible: we measured this, here is our resolution, here is how much we trust it. A market-intelligence service sold to the people being measured has to earn belief on inspection, and this is the device that does it. No generic dashboard would ever cut an axis on purpose.

The structural revision · this one reordered the brief

I came into this stage assuming the fog seam was the signature, because §2 offers it as the candidate and it is the most striking thing in the reference. Writing the generic column out killed that. A generic dark-map brief with this reference attached would also produce the fog seam — it is the most copyable element on the page. Inheriting it well is craft, not identity.

What a generic brief would never produce is a deliberately truncated axis. It would render 4.9 as a nearly-full five-star row and call the job done — and in doing so it would rank a one-review clinic above a 385-review clinic and never notice. So the signature moved: the fog is law we obey, the vernier is the thing that is ours. Everything downstream — the data-viz section, the row anatomy, the histogram treatment — reorders around that.
