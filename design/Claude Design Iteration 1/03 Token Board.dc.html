Stage 03 Token board · the palette and the ruler

Dark is not a theme here.
Dark is the product.

There is no light mode and there will never be one. The map is the ground, the ground is near-black, and every surface in this system is defined as a quantity of light laid over it. A light theme would not be a variant — it would be a different product with a different argument.

The rule this board exists to make

Nothing downstream ever invents a value. If a screen needs a colour, a size, a radius or a duration that is not on this board, the board is wrong and gets amended — the screen does not improvise.

The contrast floor · measured, and it changed the palette

Stage 1 warned that translucent glass over a dark map is where contrast quietly fails. It does. Every pair below is a real WCAG 2.1 computation against the composited backdrop, and two of them failed — which forced a change to the text ramp rather than a note in an appendix.

What it forced · we depart from the reference here

The reference's tiny caps labels sit at roughly 40% opacity. Composited over glass on the brightest part of the map that measures 2.94 — it fails at every text size. Copying it faithfully would ship a page whose labels cannot be read by anyone with reduced contrast sensitivity, which on a product sold to doctors is not a defensible trade.

So the label floor is 62%, measuring 4.87 in the same worst case. And the delicacy the reference bought with low contrast, we buy with size, tracking and weight instead — 10 px at 0.20 em in a monospace at weight 500 reads as quiet without being faint.

Two hard consequences, both testable
reading-4 may never carry text.
At 34% it measures 2.72 on bare ground and fails both thresholds. It survives as a token only for non-informational marks: scale ticks, hairline rules, the corner affordance. Anything a user must read is barred from it.
signal is never text on glass.
4.44 over glass-on-roadbed — passes as a graphic, fails as body copy. Amber type requires bare ground or a scrim beneath it. On the map it is light, not lettering, so the rule costs nothing.
The scrim is load-bearing, not decorative.
An 0.82 ground scrim turns #2E3A38 into #0C1212 and lifts reading-2 from 5.32 to 7.35. Every floating cluster gets one, and that is now a contrast requirement rather than a style.

The fog · as four tokens, not one gradient

You asked for the fog gradient as a reusable token. It cannot be one, and Stage 1's measurement is why: the effect is a filter on the basemap plus a plate above it plus a mask that feathers the onset. Shipping it as a single gradient is the failure mode — that produces a dark panel.

Every curve ends flat. No overshoot, no bounce, no cubic-bezier with a y value above 1. "Weighted, premium, never springy" is enforceable as: the fourth control point is never greater than 1.

The slight delay · it is a delay, not a duration
delay-considered 90ms

The premium feel you describe is a hesitation before a large motion, not a slow motion. Slowing the travel makes an interface feel sluggish; pausing 90 ms then travelling at full speed makes it feel considered.

So dot → list is 90ms delay + 520ms travel, not a 610 ms scroll. Applied only to motions that cross a boundary; hover states get no delay at all.

Reduced motion · instant, not merely faster

Under prefers-reduced-motion every duration and delay in this table becomes 0 ms and the dot pulse stops entirely — states are set, not animated. Not 50% faster, not a shorter easing. The map still pans, because panning is the user's own gesture and suppressing it would break the tool.
